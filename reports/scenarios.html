Scenario-Based Analysis

Soil Carbon and Annual Nitrous-Oxide Results, 2024–2045

Authors

David LeBauer

Akash B V

Published

September 29, 2026

The scenario-based analysis compares four scenarios through December 2045. The reference uses business-as-usual management, CESM2 climate, and SSP370. Each alternative changes one dimension relative to the reference.

Soil-carbon context

The negative reference-scenario change may include adjustment from a non-stable initial state. Scenario effects are therefore evaluated primarily as matched differences from the reference trajectory.

Scenario effects relative to the reference

Management retains an average of 1.55 Tg C more than the reference trajectory by December 2045. Changing the climate model produces little change in the statewide mean but different annual- and woody-crop responses. SSP585 reduces soil carbon by an additional 3.71 Tg C relative to the reference.

Scenario soil-carbon responses

Annual N2O-N

Scenario N2O-N is integrated over each calendar year and reported in kg N ha-1 yr-1. The current annual comparison covers the reference and management scenarios; it is an interim two-scenario result rather than the finalized four-scenario annual-gas product.

The management mean is higher than the reference in most years, although the same-member range commonly crosses zero. Methane is not shown here; methane analysis will focus on crop fields with at least one rice season.

Related practice-change benchmark

A separate practice-change benchmark compares paired treatment and reference simulations at statewide design points with published synthesis estimates. Cover crops, reduced tillage, zero tillage, and organic amendment increased modeled soil carbon. The cover-crop and tillage effects were roughly half the synthesis estimates, while the organic-amendment effect was about one quarter. The design includes annual- and woody-crop systems, but uses one fixed parameterization for 2016–2023 and is distinct from both the inventory and the 2024–2045 ensemble scenario analysis reported here.
